# Facilitator Guide — Brain Explorer Kids

For a teacher, librarian, after-school leader, troop/club volunteer, or parent
running this with a group of 6–12 learners aged 8–13.

Everything here needs **no accounts, no installs, and no internet after the first
load**. Open `demo-pages/kids-index.html` (or the live site) and you are running.

---

## Before the session (10 minutes, once)

1. Open the page on the device learners will use. Let it load fully once so the
   browser caches it.
2. Check the sound toggle in the header — the platform speaks instructions aloud
   for learners who need it. Decide now whether to leave it on.
3. Read the **Parents** tab yourself. It holds the privacy statement, the
   age-banded objectives, and the badge list you will be asked about.
4. Decide your pairs. Mixed-ability pairs work better than ability-grouped ones
   here, because the modules reward explanation, not speed.

## Session shape

Three formats are given. Pick by available time.

### Format A — 45-minute single module

| Min | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Hook: "Your brain is a network. Prove it." | Ask for two words they think are connected. Write both on the board. |
| 5–10 | Whole-group demo of **Word Web** on one screen | Activate one concept; ask them to predict what lights up next. |
| 10–30 | Pairs on the module | Circulate. Ask "why did *that* one light up?" not "did you finish?" |
| 30–38 | Pairs write one sentence: *"Activation spreads to ___ because ___"* | This sentence is the assessment. Collect it. |
| 38–45 | Two pairs read out; class decides which explanation is strongest and why | |

### Format B — 90-minute, two modules

Same as Format A for the first 45 minutes with **Word Web**, then repeat the shape
with **Neuron Lab**. In the second block, the hook becomes: *"The web was ideas. Now
it's cells. What's the same?"* The closing sentence becomes *"Neurons fire when ___
because ___."*

### Format C — Four-session sequence (recommended for a club or troop)

| Session | Module | Big question | Closing artefact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Word Web | How are ideas connected? | One-sentence activation claim |
| 2 | Neuron Lab | How do brain cells talk? | Labelled screenshot of a spike train with two observations |
| 3 | Thought Paths | What is the fastest route, and is fastest always best? | Two routes compared, with the trade-off named |
| 4 | Brain Power Meter | Why does learning feel hard, and when is hard good? | Personal "one thing that overloads me" + one fix |

The sequence is deliberate: it moves from *representation* → *mechanism* →
*algorithm* → *self-regulation*. That progression is the one the learning-science
literature supports, and it ends on metacognition, which is the part that transfers.

## Facilitation moves that matter

**Praise the process, not the person.** "You tested three routes before deciding" beats
"you're so smart". The platform's own copy uses growth framing; keep your language
consistent with it.

**Ask for predictions before clicks.** Prediction-then-reveal is what converts a
toy into an experiment. Every module has an obvious prediction point.

**Normalise the stuck moment.** When a pair is stuck, say the true thing: *"This is
the part where the algorithm gets interesting."* Struggle attributed to the task is
protective; struggle attributed to the child is not.

**Name the person, not the category.** When you mention a scientist, say what she
figured out and how. Never "a famous female scientist" — that frames her gender as
the achievement.

**Watch for the hand-off pattern.** In pair work, one learner often takes the mouse
and the other watches. Intervene by assigning roles that swap on a timer (Driver /
Recorder), not by exhorting them to share.

## Differentiation

| Need | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Reading below grade level | Turn the speaker on; the platform reads instructions aloud |
| Visual sensitivity | The page honours `prefers-reduced-motion`; also set OS-level reduce-motion |
| Low contrast needs | The page honours `prefers-contrast: high` |
| Keyboard-only or motor access needs | Every module has toolbar buttons that drive the canvas; Tab reaches them. The canvas itself is not required. |
| Learners who finish early | Ask them to break it: "what input makes the model behave *wrong*?" Finding the failure mode is the highest-value extension |
| Learners who disengage | Offer the Recorder role first, then hand over the Driver role with a scripted first click |

## What to avoid

- Do not front-load vocabulary. The modules introduce terms in context; a
  definition list beforehand turns an exploration into a lecture.
- Do not turn the badge list into a leaderboard. Badges here mark exploration
  milestones, not ranking, and comparing them re-creates the competition that
  the design is trying to avoid.
- Do not collect learner names into the tool. There is nowhere to put them, and
  that is on purpose.

## Materials

- One device per pair (tablet or laptop). The layout is responsive down to phone
  width but pair work needs a shared screen.
- Paper and pencil for the closing sentence. Writing it by hand slows the pair down
  enough to actually argue about the wording.
- Optional: printed `docs/ASSESSMENT-RUBRIC.md`, one per pair, for self-check.
